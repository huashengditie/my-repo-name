1. BACKGROUND AND MAJOR
================================================================

Dr. Vassiliy Tsytsarev holds a Ph.D. in Neuroscience from Saint Petersburg
State University, Russia. He began his international research career in
Japan, spending roughly seven years at RIKEN's Brain Science Institute and
the Human Brain Research Center in Kyoto, where he focused on auditory
neuroscience using intrinsic optical imaging and voltage-sensitive dye (VSD)
imaging. He subsequently moved to the United States, holding research
positions at several institutions including the University of Maryland
School of Medicine, before joining Johns Hopkins University, where he now
serves as an instructor in the Engineering for Professionals program.

His disciplinary home sits at the intersection of neuroscience,
neurotechnology, optical/biomedical imaging, and biomedical engineering —
he is trained as a neuroscientist but works with the instrumentation and
signal-processing toolkit of an engineer, which is exactly the profile
suited to teaching in an engineering (rather than a pure life-sciences)
graduate program.

He has authored or co-authored 60+ peer-reviewed articles and multiple
book/handbook chapters.

================================================================
2. MAJOR AREAS OF FOCUS
================================================================

- Epilepsy and seizure research — mechanisms, detection, and imaging of
  epileptic activity in animal (primarily rodent) models.
- Functional brain imaging using optical methods: intrinsic optical
  signal (IOS) imaging, voltage-sensitive dye (VSD) imaging, laser
  speckle contrast imaging, and photoacoustic/optical coherence
  tomography (dual-contrast, high spatiotemporal resolution).
- Neurovascular coupling — how neural activity relates to local blood
  flow/metabolism changes, a core mechanism underlying many functional
  imaging signals.
- Neural circuits and the dopamine system.
- Traumatic brain injury and neural regeneration.
- Emerging neurotechnology and neurophotonics (optical sensors, novel
  imaging modalities for brain monitoring).

Representative publications:
- "Optical Imaging of Epileptic Seizures," Handbook of Neuroengineering,
  Springer (2022) — covers intrinsic optical imaging and VSD imaging
  techniques for seizure visualization.
- "In vivo laser speckle contrast imaging of 4-aminopyridine- or
  pentylenetetrazole-induced seizures," APL Bioengineering (2023).
- "Photoacoustic and optical coherence tomography of epilepsy with high
  temporal and spatial resolution and dual optical contrasts," Journal
  of Neuroscience Methods (2013).
- "Transcranial Dynamic Fluorescence Imaging for the Study of Epileptic
  Seizures."

================================================================
3. RELATION TO THE EEG ELIPSY PROJECT
================================================================

This project (EEG Elipsy) builds a patient-independent seizure detection
pipeline on the CHB-MIT scalp EEG dataset: EDF ingestion, seizure
annotation parsing, windowing, patient-independent fold splitting,
spectral feature extraction, and classical-ML baselines (with a CNN
pipeline planned next). The core scientific object in both his lab and
this project is the same thing — the electrical/optical signature of
epileptic seizure activity in the brain — but captured through different
sensing modalities:

- Dr. Tsytsarev's work reads seizures through OPTICAL signals (VSD,
  intrinsic signal, laser speckle, photoacoustic/OCT) recorded directly
  from cortex in animal models, at high spatial resolution but typically
  in controlled, invasive, single-animal settings.
- This project reads seizures through scalp EEG, a purely ELECTRICAL,
  non-invasive, clinical-scale signal, across many human patients, with
  the goal of automated, generalizable (patient-independent) detection.

The two modalities are complementary views of the same underlying
neurovascular/neuroelectric event: EEG captures the electrical
discharge directly and near-instantaneously; his optical methods
(especially those tied to neurovascular coupling) capture the slower
hemodynamic/metabolic response that electrical activity triggers. A
researcher who studies both modalities' relationship is well positioned
to evaluate whether a purely electrical (EEG-based) detection pipeline
is capturing the same seizure onset/propagation dynamics that optical
imaging shows at the cortical-surface level in animal models — this is
one of the most direct and substantive points of overlap between his
expertise and this project's subject matter.

4. HOW THIS PROJECT COULD BE RELEVANT/HELPFUL TO HIS WORK
================================================================

- Cross-modal validation: The spectral/temporal seizure signatures this
  project extracts from EEG (band power features, onset/offset timing
  from annotations, patient-independent generalization results) could
  serve as a human-scale, non-invasive electrical reference against
  which his animal optical-imaging findings on seizure propagation and
  neurovascular coupling could be compared or contextualized.
- Signal-processing and ML methodology transfer: This project's
  pipeline architecture — batched spectral feature extraction (Welch's
  method), rigorous per-fold scaler fitting to avoid leakage,
  patient-independent splitting — is a template that could be adapted to
  his optical imaging data (e.g., automated seizure event detection in
  laser speckle or VSD recordings), which currently likely relies more
  on manual or simpler thresholding approaches.
- Multimodal seizure detection framework: If his lab pursues combined
  EEG + optical imaging experiments (a natural next step in
  neurovascular-coupling research), this project's EEG feature-extraction
  and classification codebase could serve as the EEG-side component of
  a multimodal pipeline, with his optical data providing the
  complementary hemodynamic channel.
- Teaching value: As an EP instructor, he may find this project useful
  as a concrete, well-documented example of an end-to-end biomedical
  signal-processing/ML pipeline (data auditing, quality gates, feature
  engineering, patient-independent evaluation) for coursework or student
  projects on biomedical signal analysis.

================================================================
5. SUMMARY
================================================================

Dr. Tsytsarev is a neuroscientist-engineer whose career centers on
optical imaging of epileptic seizures and neurovascular coupling in
animal models. This project's EEG-based, patient-independent seizure
detection pipeline studies the same phenomenon (epileptic seizures)
through a different, clinically dominant sensing modality (scalp EEG)
at human scale. The overlap is substantive rather than superficial:
both efforts are ultimately trying to characterize when and how seizure
activity manifests and propagates, using rigorous signal-processing and
quantitative methods — making him a strong domain mentor for grounding
this project's biological/clinical interpretation, and making this
project a plausible methodological or comparative asset for his own
research program.
